# Your Google Services List Is a Sales Page: How to Write Services That Bring Calls

Most business owners fill in their Google Business Profile services list in about four minutes. They type the category names Google suggests, click save, and never look at it again. That's a waste, because the services list is one of the few parts of your profile a searcher actually reads before deciding to call — and it quietly helps Google match you to searches that never mention your business name.

## What should a service entry actually say?

A good service entry has three parts: the name your customer would type, a short description in plain words, and a price or a "from" price where you can give one honestly. "Emergency plumber" is a category. "Same-day water heater replacement, 40 and 50 gallon, gas and electric" is a service a real person searches for at 9pm with water on the floor.

Write the description the way you'd explain the job on the phone. Two or three sentences is plenty. Say what's included, who it's for, and anything that separates your version of the job from the next listing's.

## Does the price field matter if you don't want to commit?

Yes — and you don't have to commit to an exact number. A "from $X" price does two jobs. It tells the searcher you're in their budget range, and it filters out the callers who were never going to pay your rate. Hiding the price completely doesn't make serious buyers call; it usually makes them call the competitor whose price they could see. If your pricing genuinely depends on the job, say so in the description and still give the starting point.

## The mistakes that keep services invisible

- **Copying the category list.** Google already knows your categories. Services written in the customer's own words catch the searches categories miss.
- **One service, twenty locations.** A service description stuffed with city names reads as spam and helps nobody.
- **Services you don't actually offer.** Every service on the list is a promise. If you don't do the work, the call wastes your time and the review risk lands on you.
- **Never updating it.** Prices change, you drop a service, you add a better one. A list from two years ago sells a business that no longer exists.

## A 20-minute fix

Open your profile, read every service out loud, and cut anything you wouldn't say to a customer standing in front of you. Rewrite each one around the problem it solves, add the "from" price, and check the list again whenever your prices or offer change. It's unglamorous work. It's also the part of the profile that turns a view into a phone call.
