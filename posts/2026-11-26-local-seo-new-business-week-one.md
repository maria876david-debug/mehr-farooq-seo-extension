# Local SEO for a New Business: What to Do in Week One

A new business does not need a big marketing budget in its first week. It needs to be findable. Most local customers will never see your website first — they will see your Google Business Profile, or nothing at all. Week one is about getting the basics right so the next months have something to build on.

## Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

Start with the profile itself. Claim it, then fill in every field honestly: the exact business name, the right primary category, your real opening hours, your phone number, and the area you actually serve. Add real photos of your work, your shopfront, or your team — not stock photos. A half-finished profile looks unfinished to customers, and Google shows complete profiles more often than empty ones.

## Make sure Maps puts you in the right place

Check the pin on the map yourself. If it sits on the wrong street, or in the wrong part of town, fix it before you tell anyone to look you up. A customer who follows the map to the wrong place rarely tries twice. While you are there, check that the name, address and phone number on your website match the profile exactly — the same spelling, the same number, everywhere.

## Put one honest page on your website

You do not need ten pages in week one. You need one page that says clearly what you do, where you do it, and how to contact you. One page, written for a real customer in your town, beats a website full of pages that say nothing. Add your service area in plain words — the towns and neighbourhoods you actually travel to — and a phone number people can tap.

## Ask your first customers for a review

The hardest review to get is the first one, and the best time to ask is right after a job well done. Ask in person, send a short message with the link, and make it easy. Do not offer a discount for a review and never write one yourself — a handful of honest reviews from real customers is worth more than a page full of praise nobody believes.

## What not to do in week one

Do not buy a list of directory listings, do not stuff your business name with keywords, and do not promise customers things you cannot deliver. Local SEO is slow work: the businesses that rank in six months are usually the ones that did the boring basics properly in week one and kept doing them every week after.
